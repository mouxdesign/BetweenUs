Wandia Mugo grew up in Nairobi navigating a financial world dictated by urgency.

Mobile money systems like M-Pesa had made it easier to send and receive cash without carrying it physically. But the deeper concept of money still felt foreign to her. "Before late 2024, everything was just up in the air. I didn't even have a grasp of personal finances, things like budgeting or tracking your money."

That changed in late 2024, when Wandia was introduced to Bitcoin.

"It was mind blowing. I feel like when I found Bitcoin, it not only gave me this way to connect with people worldwide, it made me want to learn more about finances and money in general." She started showing up: book clubs, local events, any resource she could find.

Then something made it real and everything shifted. She started working as a developer and received her first paycheck entirely in Bitcoin.

:::pullquote
I feel like when I found Bitcoin, it not only gave me this way to connect with people worldwide, it made me want to learn more about finances and money in general.
:::

"I looked at that balance in my Bitnob wallet and a lightbulb just went off. I thought, okay, I need to figure out how to use this money in my context because I need to live."

Kenya's mobile money had already taught her that her phone could double as a digital wallet. When Bitcoin entered her life, it felt familiar. What she didn't expect was how much it would change her relationship with money itself.

"In fiat, I had never earned enough. You earn and you think it's enough, and by next month the price of petrol has hiked, and you're left wondering how you're supposed to pay for the same things." But this was different. She'd been paid while Bitcoin was climbing, and she watched her balance move in a way fiat never had. "I didn't even understand the mechanics of what was happening, which is why I started learning about the markets. When you zoom out, you see the gains. I had never seen anything like it."

:::pullquote

Then theoretical became practical. She had to figure out how to use it in her everyday life, how to spend it. "I started using Tando, an off-ramp here in Kenya that lets you perform M-Pesa services via Bitcoin." Now she lives on what is being called the Bitcoin standard. "Because I get paid in Bitcoin, I don't even touch fiat. I go straight to the shop and use Tando. Whether it's groceries or buying things on Instagram, I just use Bitcoin. It's become my standard of living."

For Wandia, this is bigger than a hedge against inflation or a convenient payment rail. "It's a philosophy," she says. "I think about how this tool can liberate marginalized people, the women in my family who don't have to disclose their financial information to a bank or a husband. They can use this money freely. We are no longer thinking about borders, governments, or censorship."

That freedom reshaped how she sees herself. She used to feel she had to fit into a single professional box to be valued. Now she lets herself be everything she wants to be at once. "I can be an artist. I can offer astrological services to anyone in the world, and they can pay me via Bitcoin. I don't need transformation or permission from anyone."

Her days run on structure. She is the founder of Bitcoin Plain Talk, a Bitcoin glossary that breaks down Bitcoin jargon into local African languages while creating base data for local language LLM training, and the Head of Operations for Bitcoin DADA. Yoga, manifestation, water, and then work. She finds a quiet center before diving into the high-energy world of Bitcoin in the evening, once the rest of the world goes quiet enough for her to think.

:::pullquote
When you show them, when you buy them a drink or some fries using BTC, that's when the light bulb clicks.
:::

Wandia enjoys teaching people how to use Bitcoin, and she has learned that a demonstration beats an argument. When she sits with her family, she no longer lectures them about decentralization. She simply sends a transaction and shows them the result. "When you talk to people, they don't believe you. But when you show them, when you buy them a drink or some fries using BTC, that's when the light bulb clicks. That's when they want to know how to use it in their own lives."

Her advice to skeptics is the same thing that convinced her family. "Show them practically how this can assist you in your life. Can we go buy some groceries? Can I buy you a drink? Can I buy you some fries?"
